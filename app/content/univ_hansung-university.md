## What Hansung University Is Known For

Hansung University (한성대학교) is a private university in Seongbuk-gu, central-north Seoul, best known within Korea for two things: its fashion design program, which regularly places graduates in Korean apparel and beauty companies, and its IT convergence departments, which grew out of a decades-long push to link coursework directly to industry placement through Korea's LINC (Leaders in Industry-University Cooperation) initiative. It is not one of Seoul's "SKY" or top-tier research universities, and it does not try to be — its identity is built around practical, employment-oriented majors, a compact campus, and tuition that runs noticeably lower than in-city competitors like Sogang or Sungkyunkwan. For international students, that combination makes Hansung a realistic option for TOPIK-track admission, vocational-adjacent majors, and a genuinely local, non-touristy neighborhood experience.

## Campus Footprint and Neighborhood

The main campus sits at 116 Samseongyo-ro 16-gil, Seongbuk-gu, wedged into a hillside residential district between Hansung University Station and Hanseong Univ. Station area shops. Unlike sprawling campuses such as Korea University or Kyung Hee, Hansung's academic buildings are stacked vertically across a steep slope, connected by outdoor stairs and a few internal elevators — worth knowing before committing to a dorm assignment far from your department building. The surrounding Samseon-dong and Donam-dong blocks are quiet, low-rise, and dominated by student housing (원룸), small restaurants, and independent cafés rather than large commercial strips, which keeps daily living costs down compared to Hongdae- or Gangnam-adjacent campuses.

Tuition at Hansung is set per college rather than a single flat rate, and design/practical majors run higher than humanities or social science tracks. Design-track lab and materials fees (fabric, printing, model-making supplies) are billed separately from tuition and can add 200,000-500,000 KRW per semester depending on the studio course.

International enrollment at Hansung concentrates in a handful of departments rather than spreading evenly across the university. The table below reflects where applicant interest and existing international cohorts are strongest, based on program English-track availability and industry linkage.

Most lecture courses outside Global MICE and select IT electives are delivered in Korean, so a TOPIK Level 3-4 minimum is realistic for direct-enrollment applicants, with Level 4+ strongly preferred for humanities and business majors where reading load is heavier.

## Visa and Admission Track Specifics

Degree-seeking international students at Hansung apply for the D-2 student visa, while those entering through the university's Korean Language Institute first hold a D-4 visa before transitioning to D-2 upon degree admission. Required documents follow the standard Korean university pattern: high school (or prior degree) transcripts and certificates apostilled or consular-authenticated, a financial guarantee showing roughly USD 20,000 (or KRW equivalent, often shown as a bank balance certificate held for the preceding months), a passport-size photo, and either a TOPIK certificate or proof of enrollment in Hansung's own language track. Hansung's admissions office runs at least two annual intakes (spring, March start; fall, September start), and international applicants should budget 3-4 months before the intended semester for document preparation, embassy visa interview scheduling, and the Alien Registration Card (ARC) process after arrival.

## Cost of Living Around Seongbuk-gu

Because Hansung sits outside Seoul's high-rent core, monthly living costs run lower than campuses in Sinchon or Gangnam. A single room (원룸) near Hansung University Station typically runs 400,000-600,000 KRW per month including a modest deposit, versus 700,000+ near central-Seoul campuses. On-campus dormitory space is limited and competitive, so most continuing international students end up in private housing within a 10-15 minute walk of campus. Food costs at nearby student-district restaurants average 7,000-9,000 KRW per meal, and the Hansung University Station area on Line 4 gives direct, no-transfer access to Dongdaemun, Myeongdong, and Seoul Station within 20-30 minutes — useful for part-time work under the standard 20-hour/week D-2 visa limit.

## Scholarships Worth Checking Before You Apply

Hansung offers a tuition-reduction scholarship tier for incoming international students based on prior academic GPA and TOPIK score, generally reducing first-year tuition by 30-50%, with renewal contingent on maintaining a minimum GPA (commonly around 3.0/4.5) each subsequent semester. These awards are department-administered rather than centrally guaranteed, so applicants should confirm exact thresholds and renewal terms directly with their target department's admissions coordinator rather than relying on the general international office page, since criteria have shifted between intake cycles in recent years.
