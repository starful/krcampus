## Where Dong-Eui Fits Among Busan's Private Universities

Dong-Eui University (동의대학교) is a mid-to-large private university founded in 1978, sitting in Busanjin-gu on the northern edge of Busan's Seomyeon commercial district — one of the busiest downtown areas in South Korea's second-largest city. Unlike universities pushed to the outskirts, Dong-Eui's main Gaya campus is a 10-15 minute subway ride from Seomyeon Station (Busan Metro Lines 1 and 2), which matters for international students weighing part-time work options, cost of living, and social life against a purely academic ranking. A second campus in Gimhae hosts some health-science and arts programs, but most international-track majors run out of the Busanjin-gu site.

## Academic Identity: Pharmacy and Health Sciences as Anchors

What separates Dong-Eui from many similarly sized private Busan schools is its College of Pharmacy, established in 2011 through the government's regional pharmacy-school expansion — a six-year licensure track that is difficult to get into even for domestic students, so international applicants should treat it as a long-term goal rather than a direct-entry option. The university has built a broader health-sciences cluster around it: nursing, physical therapy, occupational therapy, and health administration all draw steady enrollment because Busan's aging population and hospital density create real local job demand after graduation. Engineering and IT convergence majors (mechanical, electronic, computer software) form the second core, supported by Busan's manufacturing and shipping-adjacent industries.

Note on admission fees: following a 2021-2023 Korean government policy phase-out, Dong-Eui — like nearly all domestic universities — no longer charges a separate 입학금 (matriculation fee) to newly admitted students, domestic or international. Budget instead for the Korean Immersion Program fee if you enter via the language track, plus mandatory National Health Insurance enrollment (roughly 120,000-140,000 KRW/month) once you hold a D-2 or D-4 visa.

## Language Track and the D-4 to D-2 Pathway

Students without TOPIK Level 3 or higher typically enter through Dong-Eui's Korean Language Education Center on a D-4-1 visa, completing up to four 10-week terms before transitioning into a degree program. Degree-track international admission generally asks for TOPIK 3 (some majors require TOPIK 4, particularly pharmacy and nursing given the technical vocabulary), or equivalent English-medium eligibility for the limited English-taught course offerings. Once accepted into a bachelor's program, students convert from D-4 to D-2 status; the university's International Education Center handles the Certificate of Admission and coordinates with the Busan Immigration Office for the visa change, which typically needs to be filed before the D-4 expiration date to avoid a gap in legal status.

## Housing Reality Near a Downtown Campus

Because the campus sits close to Seomyeon rather than in a rural university town, off-campus studio rent runs higher than at outer-Busan or Gyeongsang-province schools — expect 350,000-500,000 KRW/month for a basic officetel within a 15-minute commute, versus 200,000-300,000 KRW in more remote areas. This pushes most new international students toward the on-campus dormitory in year one, where costs land closer to 700,000-900,000 KRW per semester including a basic meal plan, with priority booking generally given to freshmen and exchange students. Budget-conscious students often move off-campus after their first year once they've built a local network for splitting rent.

## Work and Post-Graduation Considerations

D-2 visa holders can apply for part-time work permission (시간제 취업허가) after one semester of enrollment, capped at 25-30 hours weekly during term time and unrestricted during official vacation periods — Seomyeon's dense retail and hospitality sector makes part-time jobs comparatively easy to find relative to smaller university towns. Graduates from Dong-Eui's engineering, IT, and healthcare programs who want to stay in Korea typically pursue the D-10 job-seeking visa immediately after graduation, then transition to E-7 (specific activity) employment status if hired by a Busan-based manufacturer, hospital, or IT firm — a pathway the university's career center actively supports through employer partnerships in the Busan-Gyeongnam industrial belt rather than assuming most graduates head to Seoul.

## Application Documents Specific to Dong-Eui

Beyond the standard diploma, transcript, and financial proof (bank balance certificate typically requiring the equivalent of one year's tuition plus living expenses, held for a minimum holding period before application), Dong-Eui's International Education Center asks for a self-introduction essay and study plan in Korean or English depending on track, plus a health checkup form for dormitory applicants. Pharmacy and nursing applicants should expect an additional interview stage given seat scarcity, conducted either in person or via video call for overseas applicants.
