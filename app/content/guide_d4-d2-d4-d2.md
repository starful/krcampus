# D4에서 D2 비자로 전환하는 법: Switching from a D-4 Visa to a D-2 Student Visa in Korea

Many international students in Korea start on a D-4 (language/training) visa while studying at a university language institute, then later move into a full degree program. That transition requires switching your visa status from D-4 to D-2 (academic student visa). This guide covers how the process works, what it costs, and mistakes to avoid.

## Why D-4 Students Eventually Need D-2

The D-4 visa is issued for Korean language programs (어학당) or other non-degree courses, typically up to 1 year per renewal, renewable up to 2 years (sometimes longer at certain universities). It does not allow enrollment in a bachelor's, master's, or doctoral program. Once you receive an admission offer from a degree program — at the same university or elsewhere — you must change your status to D-2 before the semester begins. Immigration won't let you attend degree classes on D-4 status, and schools won't finalize registration without the correct visa.

## Eligibility Requirements Before You Apply

Confirm you meet these baseline requirements before filing:

- A formal **certificate of admission (입학허가서)** from a Korean degree program (associate, bachelor's, master's, or PhD)
- Required Korean or English proficiency (many Korean-taught majors require TOPIK Level 3+; English-taught tracks may waive this)
- No unauthorized part-time work violations or overstays on your D-4 status
- A still-valid D-4 visa at application time — if it's lapsed, resolve the extension/re-entry issue first
- Proof of financial support (bank statement, scholarship letter, or sponsor's documents)

If you studied at a language institute affiliated with your target university, admissions often streamline the process since your records are already on file.

## Document Checklist for the Change of Status

Applications go through **HiKorea** (hikorea.go.kr), online or in person at your local immigration office. Typical requirements:

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Application form (통합신청서) | Downloadable from HiKorea |
| Passport & Alien Registration Card (ARC) | Original + copy |
| Certificate of Admission | From the university's admissions office |
| Enrollment/Completion Certificate (D-4 program) | Shows language course history |
| Academic transcripts | From D-4 institute and prior schools |
| Proof of tuition payment or deposit | Bank receipt |
| Financial proof | Bank balance (often ~10M KRW equivalent) or scholarship proof |
| Passport-size photo | Taken within 6 months |
| NHIS enrollment proof | Required since 2021 for most visa types |

Bring originals and photocopies — immigration offices are strict, and a missing document can delay things by weeks since slots fill fast near semester start.

## Step-by-Step Application Process

1. **Get your Certificate of Admission** once you pass the entrance exam/interview and pay any deposit.
2. **Book a HiKorea appointment** at the office covering your registered address — Seoul, Gyeonggi, and Busan are often booked 2-4 weeks out during peak season (Dec-Jan for spring, Jun-Jul for fall).
3. **Submit your application** online via HiKorea or in person. Many universities review documents first to catch errors.
4. **Pay the processing fee** — typically 60,000 KRW (confirm current amount on HiKorea).
5. **Wait for processing** — usually 2-4 weeks, longer near semester start.
6. **Receive your new ARC** reflecting D-2 status, or a pickup notification.

## Timing and Costs at a Glance

Apply at the right time — too early (before admission is confirmed) or too late (after D-4 expires) both cause problems.

| Item | Typical Value |
|---|---|
| Processing fee | ~60,000 KRW |
| Processing time | 2–4 weeks (longer in peak season) |
| Recommended application window | 1–2 months before semester start |
| D-4 validity needed | Must not be expired at submission |
| D-2 initial visa length | Usually 1 year, renewable per semester/year |
| TOPIK requirement (Korean-taught majors) | Often Level 3+ |

Submit soon after your admission letter arrives, not the week before classes, to leave a buffer for extra document requests.

## Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

- **Letting D-4 expire mid-process**: Apply for an extension first, or file well before expiry.
- **Incomplete financial documentation**: Officers often reject bank statements that don't meet the minimum balance or aren't recent (most require statements within 1-3 months).
- **Skipping university verification**: Some schools require an extra "confirmation of enrollment" letter beyond the admission certificate — check with your university.
- **Assuming online submission is always available**: Not every office processes every visa type online; some require in-person submission for first-time D-2 conversions.
- **Forgetting health insurance enrollment**: Missing mandatory NHIS subscription can stall ARC issuance even after approval.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I work part-time once I switch to D-2?**
Yes, but you need separate part-time work permission (시간제취업허가), and hour limits depend on TOPIK level and semester status (vacations allow more hours).

**What happens if my D-2 application is denied?**
You can reapply with corrected documents, but must keep valid D-4 status while appealing or resubmitting.

**Do I need to leave Korea to switch visa types?**
No. Change of status (체류자격변경) is processed domestically through HiKorea, no exit/re-entry needed.

**Is TOPIK mandatory for every D-2 applicant?**
Depends on the program's language of instruction. Korean-medium majors commonly require TOPIK 3+; many English-taught tracks at universities like Yonsei, Korea University, or SKKU waive it — check each program's policy.

**How early should I start the process?**
Begin gathering documents as soon as you get your admission offer — ideally 2-3 months before the semester, since appointment slots and processing both lengthen in peak season.
