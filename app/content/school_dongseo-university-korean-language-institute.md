## Where Dongseo University's Korean Program Fits in Busan

Dongseo University's 한국어교육원 (Korean Language Institute, KLI) sits on the university's Sasang-gu campus in Busan, South Korea's second-largest city. Unlike Seoul-based programs competing for the same pool of applicants, Dongseo draws a large share of Southeast Asian, Central Asian, and Chinese students who want a lower cost of living, a smaller international-student ratio per classroom, and a direct pipeline into Dongseo's undergraduate and graduate departments — particularly its well-known film, design, and IT programs (Dongseo runs Busan's APEC-linked Busan International Film Festival partnerships, which gives the campus a distinct creative-industry identity). Students choosing between Busan and Seoul should weigh Busan's noticeably lower rent and food costs against Seoul's larger job and internship market.

## Six-Level Curriculum and Weekly Structure

The program runs six proficiency levels (Level 1 through Level 6), roughly mapped to TOPIK I and TOPIK II bands. Each level spans one ten-week quarter, with four intakes a year (March, June, September, December), so a student entering at Level 1 in March can realistically reach Level 4–5 within a year if they pass each quarter's level-up exam. Classes meet Monday through Friday, four class hours per day (about 200 hours per quarter), split between grammar/reading in the morning and speaking/listening practice in the afternoon. Class size is capped near 15–18 students, smaller than several Seoul mega-programs, which the institute markets as its main differentiator for speaking practice.

## Tuition, Fees, and Hidden Costs

Quarterly tuition runs close to KRW 1,600,000, with a one-time application/registration fee of about KRW 100,000 for new students and a textbook fee of roughly KRW 90,000 per quarter. Compared to central Seoul programs (often KRW 1,700,000–2,000,000 per quarter), Dongseo's tuition is moderately cheaper, and the real savings show up in housing: dormitory rooms near the Busan campus run KRW 350,000–500,000 per quarter versus KRW 600,000+ for comparable Seoul university housing. Students should budget separately for the Alien Registration Card (ARC) fee (KRW 30,000), health insurance (mandatory for D-4 visa holders, roughly KRW 60,000–90,000/month), and a TOPIK exam fee (KRW 40,000 per sitting) if they plan to test.

## Campus Life on the Sasang-gu Side of Busan

Dongseo's main campus is in Sasang-gu, a district that's less touristy than Haeundae or Nampo-dong but well connected by Busan Metro Line 2 (Sasang Station) and close to Gimhae International Airport — a practical advantage for students flying in from Southeast Asia or China without connecting through Incheon. The area has budget-friendly markets, cheap Korean BBQ and dwaeji gukbap restaurants near campus, and noticeably lower average rents than Seoul's Sinchon or Hongdae international-student corridors. Dongseo also operates a second campus, Centum Campus, closer to Haeundae Beach and Busan's convention and startup district, which some KLI cultural events use for field trips.

## Dormitory, Housing, and the D-4 Visa Process

KLI students issued a D-4 (general trainee/language student) visa can apply for on-campus dormitory housing on a first-come basis each term; capacity is limited, so students admitted for the March intake should apply for housing at the same time they submit visa documents. Typical D-4 visa steps: (1) receive the standard of admission and certificate of enrollment from KLI, (2) apply for a visa issuance number or D-4 visa at a Korean embassy/consulate in the home country, (3) enter Korea and register for the Alien Registration Card within 90 days at the Busan immigration office, and (4) renew the visa each term by showing continued enrollment and a minimum attendance rate (generally 80%+). Students who fall below the attendance threshold risk visa renewal problems, so KLI enforces attendance more strictly than some casual academies.

## TOPIK Outcomes and Progression Pathways

KLI runs supplementary TOPIK prep sessions ahead of the official exam dates (administered separately by the National Institute for International Education), with mock tests built into Level 4–6 coursework. Students targeting direct admission into Dongseo's undergraduate programs typically need TOPIK 3 for most majors and TOPIK 4 for competitive departments like film and design; graduate-track applicants usually need TOPIK 4–5 depending on the department. The institute publishes a rough completer pass rate for TOPIK II 3급+ in the 70–80% range among students who complete Level 4 or above, though this varies by cohort and starting level.

## Practical Notes for Applicants

Application documents typically include a completed application form, copies of the passport and a bank statement showing sufficient funds (usually the KRW equivalent of about USD 10,000 or more, though exact figures depend on nationality and current immigration guidance), a health check or TB test certificate for applicants from certain countries, and the highest diploma or its equivalent. Deadlines generally fall about two months before each term's start (roughly early January for March intake, early April for June intake). Because Busan's international-student office is smaller than Seoul university equivalents, response times for document review can be faster in practice, but applicants should still start the visa paperwork early to avoid missing the term start date.
