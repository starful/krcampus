## A Language Institute Rooted in Korea's Food and Craft Capital

Jeonju University (전주대학교) sits in Wansan-gu, Jeonju — the Jeollabuk-do city UNESCO named a "City of Gastronomy" and the acknowledged birthplace of bibimbap. Unlike Korean-language hubs in Seoul or Busan, Jeonju offers international students a slower-paced, culturally dense environment: the 700-year-old Jeonju Hanok Village, hundreds of traditional hanji (paper) and craft workshops, and a food scene built around the city's own agricultural region. The university's International Education Institute (국제교육원) runs the Korean Language Program (한국어연수과정) for exchange students, D-4 visa holders preparing for degree study, and short-term learners. Because Jeonju has a smaller foreign resident population than the capital, students are pushed to use Korean outside class far more than in Seoul, while paying noticeably less for housing and food.

## Korean Language Program: Levels and Weekly Structure

The program follows the standard six-level system mapped to TOPIK bands, delivered in roughly 10-week terms four times per year (spring, summer, fall, winter). Placement testing happens at intake; students entering with prior TOPIK scores can skip directly to their level.

| Level | TOPIK Band | Weekly Hours | Core Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Pre-TOPIK I | 20 hrs | Hangeul, greetings, survival grammar |
| Level 2 | TOPIK I (2급) | 20 hrs | Daily conversation, basic writing |
| Level 3 | TOPIK II (3급) | 20 hrs | Intermediate grammar, short essays |
| Level 4 | TOPIK II (4급) | 20 hrs | Argumentative writing, news Korean |
| Level 5 | TOPIK II (5급) | 20 hrs | Academic reading, presentations |
| Level 6 | TOPIK II (6급) | 20 hrs | Thesis-level Korean, advanced debate |

Each term ends with a level-completion exam; students who pass Level 4 or above generally qualify for direct admission consideration into Jeonju University's undergraduate departments without a separate TOPIK certificate, though individual departments may still request TOPIK 3+ or 4+ scores.

Jeonju's cost of living runs well below Seoul: a shared studio near campus typically rents for 300,000-450,000 KRW/month, and a full meal in the old town rarely exceeds 10,000 KRW, thanks to the city's produce-driven food culture.

## Degree Programs Beyond the Language Track

Once language requirements are met, Jeonju University offers degree admission across colleges that reflect the region's identity as much as academic breadth. The university is nationally recognized for two programs in particular: its Taekwondo-focused Sports Science track, which trains members of internationally touring demonstration teams, and its Culture & Tourism College, which draws directly on Jeonju's status as a UNESCO gastronomy city and hanok heritage site.

- College of Culture & Tourism (culinary arts, tourism management, cultural content)
- College of Sports Science (Taekwondo, exercise science, sports management)
- College of Engineering & ICT (computer engineering, robotics, ICT convergence)
- College of Social Sciences (public administration, media & communication, social welfare)
- College of Theology (reflecting the university's Presbyterian founding in 1964)
- College of Arts (fine arts, music, design)

International applicants to these colleges typically need TOPIK 3 (humanities/social sciences) or TOPIK 4+ (competitive programs like media or tourism management), plus standard academic transcripts and a statement of purpose.

## Hanok Village Campus Life and the Wanju-Jeonju Setting

The main campus is a short bus ride from Jeonju Hanok Village, giving international students unusually direct access to traditional Korean architecture, hanbok rental shops, and craft studios as part of daily life rather than a weekend trip. Jeonju itself sits in a valley between the Jeollabuk-do plains and low mountains, giving it a climate slightly milder than Seoul's in winter. The city lacks Seoul's subway system, so students rely on buses and, increasingly, bike-share for local transit; intercity buses and a KTX-linked rail connection (via Iksan Station, roughly 15 minutes from Jeonju by local transfer) put Seoul within about two and a half hours door-to-door. On-campus dormitory space for international students is limited and allocated by application date, so most Korean Language Program students arrange off-campus housing through the International Education Institute's partner-housing list in their first term.

## Visa Process and Practical Steps for Language-Program Applicants

Students enrolling for a full academic year (two or more consecutive terms) apply for the D-4-1 language-training visa; those on shorter exchange arrangements may use a D-4 short-term or C-3 visa depending on program length and home-country agreements. The standard sequence:

| Step | Action | Typical Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Submit application + fee to International Education Institute | 2-3 months before term start |
| 2 | Receive Certificate of Admission (표준입학허가서) | 4-6 weeks after submission |
| 3 | Apply for D-4 visa at Korean embassy/consulate | After receiving admission certificate |
| 4 | Arrive in Korea, register at local Immigration Office | Within 90 days of entry |
| 5 | Apply for Alien Registration Card (ARC) | Within 90 days of arrival |

Because Jeonju is not a major international hub, most students fly into Incheon or Gimpo and take an airport limousine bus or KTX-plus-transfer combination to reach the city — budget roughly 3-4 hours and 30,000-40,000 KRW for the full Incheon-to-Jeonju journey. Applicants should also confirm current TOPIK/GPA thresholds directly with the department, since requirements for competitive tracks like tourism management and media communication shift by admissions cycle.
