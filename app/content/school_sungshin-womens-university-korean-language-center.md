## A Small, Focused Program in the Heart of Seongbuk-gu

Sungshin Women's University Korean Language Center (성신여자대학교 한국어교육원) sits inside a mid-sized campus in Seongbuk-gu, a residential district in northern Seoul known for its quiet streets, university-town cafes, and easy subway access via Seongshin Women's University Station (Line 4). Unlike the mega-institutes at Yonsei or Sogang that enroll thousands of students per term, Sungshin caps its language program at roughly 300 students, which keeps class sizes small — typically 12 to 18 learners per section. For applicants who want more speaking time per class and closer contact with instructors, this is the program's core selling point over larger Seoul institutes.

## Curriculum Structure Across Six Levels

The Regular Program runs six proficiency levels (1 through 6), mapped loosely onto TOPIK I and TOPIK II bands. Each level lasts one ten-week quarter, with 4 hours of class per day, Monday through Friday (20 hours/week), which meets the Korean government's minimum instructional-hour requirement for D-4 language-visa holders. Levels 1-2 emphasize survival Korean, Hangul mastery, and basic grammar patterns; Levels 3-4 shift to intermediate grammar, essay writing, and presentation skills; Levels 5-6 focus on advanced reading of news articles, academic writing, and TOPIK II Level 5-6 exam strategy.

| Level | Focus | Typical TOPIK Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Hangul, survival Korean, basic grammar | TOPIK I (1-2) |
| 3-4 | Intermediate grammar, writing, presentations | TOPIK II (3-4) |
| 5-6 | Advanced reading, academic writing, essays | TOPIK II (5-6) |

## Quarterly Admission Calendar and Fees

Sungshin admits new Regular Program students four times a year — March, June, September, and December — aligning with the standard Seoul language-institute cycle. A Short-Term Intensive Course is also offered on a rolling basis for visitors on shorter visa terms who don't need the full D-4 track. Tuition is billed per quarter rather than per semester, which makes budgeting more predictable for students paying out of pocket.

| Item | Amount (KRW) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Application/registration fee | 80,000 | One-time, non-refundable |
| Quarterly tuition | 1,650,000 | Per 10-week term, Levels 1-6 |
| Textbook fee | 80,000 | Per quarter, program-issued materials |
| Short-Term Intensive Course | 850,000 | Rolling enrollment, shorter duration |

Total first-quarter cost for a new Regular Program student is approximately 1,810,000 KRW (registration + tuition + textbooks), excluding housing and living expenses.

## Housing Near Campus: Dormitory vs. Officetel

Sungshin operates a limited on-campus dormitory reserved primarily for degree-seeking students, so language-program applicants should apply early if they want a dorm bed — spots for language learners are allocated on a first-come basis each quarter and fill quickly for the March intake. Students who don't secure a dorm room typically look at officetels and goshiwons in the surrounding Dongseon-dong and Seokgwan-dong neighborhoods, where monthly rent for a studio runs roughly 500,000-700,000 KRW plus a deposit, noticeably cheaper than areas near Hongdae or Sinchon. The center's international office provides a list of vetted nearby housing agents for incoming students who arrive without a lease already arranged.

## D-4 Visa Documentation Specific to This Program

Because Sungshin's Regular Program meets the 20-hour weekly minimum, it qualifies for D-4-1 language-training visa sponsorship. Applicants need a completed application form, passport copy, a bank statement showing a minimum balance (commonly cited around 10,000 USD equivalent, though the center confirms the exact figure each intake), the highest diploma or its equivalent, and a certificate of enrollment issued by the center after the application fee is paid. Sungshin issues the Standard Admission Letter needed for the visa application at the Korean embassy or consulate in the student's home country roughly 4-6 weeks before term start, so early document submission matters more here than at institutes with faster turnaround.

## TOPIK Preparation and Outcomes

Levels 5 and 6 fold TOPIK II mock exams into the regular weekly schedule rather than treating test prep as a separate paid add-on, which is a meaningful cost saver compared to institutes charging extra for TOPIK cram sections. The center reports that a majority of Level 6 completers sit for TOPIK II within the same quarter they finish coursework, with many achieving Level 4 or above — sufficient for most Korean undergraduate admissions and a wide range of employment visa pathways.

## Campus Culture Programs and Student Life

Beyond the classroom, the center schedules seasonal culture-experience outings — hanbok fittings, kimchi-making workshops, and trips to nearby palaces like Changdeokgung — usually once per quarter. As a historically women's university, Sungshin's broader campus culture skews toward a close-knit, safety-conscious student community, which some international students (particularly those attending alone for the first time) cite as a deciding factor over larger co-ed institutes. Language-program students are welcome to join university clubs and campus events alongside degree-seeking undergraduates, giving newcomers a faster route into Korean peer networks than programs isolated from the main student body.
