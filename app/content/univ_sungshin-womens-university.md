Sungshin Women's University (성신여자대학교) is one of Korea's oldest private women's universities, founded in 1936 in Seoul. For international students weighing a Seoul-based women's university with strong fashion, design, and business programs plus a growing AI-convergence track, here is what matters for admissions, cost, and daily life.

## Two Campuses on Line 4: Dongseon-dong and Unjeong Green Campus

Unlike many Seoul universities squeezed onto a single hillside, Sungshin runs two campuses connected by a free shuttle bus. The main **Seoul (Dongseon-dong) Campus** sits directly above Sungshin Women's University Station (Line 4, Exits 5-10) in Seongbuk-gu, putting Hyehwa, Dongdaemun, and Myeongdong within 15-20 minutes by subway. The **Unjeong Green Campus** in Suyu, Gangbuk-gu, opened in 2018 and hosts newer engineering, AI, and design labs alongside dormitories. First- and second-year students in several majors split time between the two campuses; confirm your department's assigned campus before signing a lease near either station.

## Academic Strength: Fashion, Business, and a New AI Track

Sungshin's **Department of Fashion Design** is consistently ranked among Korea's most respected, with graduates placed at domestic brands and design houses with international reach; the university's fashion shows and industry partnerships (Samsung C&T, LF, Hansae) are a genuine draw for design-focused applicants. Beyond fashion, the **College of Business Administration** and the newer **College of Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence** (launched to meet Korea's AI-workforce push) are the two fastest-growing tracks for international enrollment, alongside long-standing programs in psychology, media/communication, and early childhood education.

## Tuition, Fees, and What "Admission Fee" Means Now

Like all Korean universities, Sungshin abolished the separate one-time **입학금 (admission fee)** under a government mandate; it has been folded into general fees or removed entirely since roughly 2022, so budget primarily around per-semester tuition. Figures below are **estimates** based on recently published ranges for humanities/social-science versus studio- or lab-based majors; confirm exact current-year figures with the Office of International Affairs before transferring money.

| Item | Estimated Amount (KRW) | Note |

| Admission fee | 0 | Abolished nationwide since ~2022 |

## Korean Language Institute as an Entry Path

Applicants without TOPIK 3+ (typical undergraduate minimum) or TOPIK 4+ (common for graduate programs) frequently enter through **Sungshin's Korean Language Institute (KLI)**, a 10-week-per-term program that also issues the D-4 language-study visa. Completing KLI's upper levels both satisfies most departments' language requirements and gives you a semester or two inside Korea to sit TOPIK exams before switching to a D-2 student visa. Degree programs taught fully in English are limited at Sungshin, so realistic TOPIK targets matter more here than at bilingual-heavy schools like Yonsei or Sogang.

## Visa Timeline and Required Documents

Once admitted to a degree program, the standard path is: acceptance letter → Certificate of Admission → D-2 visa application at a Korean embassy or consulate (or a status-change if already in Korea on D-4) → Alien Registration Card (ARC) application within 90 days of entry. Sungshin's international office issues the documents needed for the visa interview, including a standardized admission letter and, for scholarship recipients, a separate confirmation letter — budget 3-6 weeks for embassy processing depending on your home country.

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Certificate of Admission | Core visa application document |
| Bank statement / financial sponsor letter | Proves ability to cover tuition and living costs |
| TOPIK certificate or English proficiency proof | Required unless entering via KLI first |
| Health insurance enrollment (after arrival) | Required for ARC and National Health Insurance |

## Housing and Cost of Living Around Seongbuk-gu and Gangbuk-gu

Seongbuk-gu (Seoul Campus) is a residential, low-rise district with cheaper *hasukjib* (boarding houses) and one-room studios than Sinchon or Gangnam — expect roughly ₩450,000–650,000 per month for a studio near Sungshin Station, versus the dorm rates listed above. Gangbuk-gu around Unjeong Green Campus is quieter and slightly cheaper still but farther from central Seoul nightlife; the inter-campus shuttle and Line 4/Ui-Sinseol Line access keep both areas commutable. Because undergraduate enrollment is women-only, Sungshin's dorms and surrounding housing networks skew toward female-only buildings, which many international students specifically look for when apartment-hunting in Seoul.

## Who Sungshin Fits Best

Sungshin suits applicants who prioritize a compact, subway-adjacent Seoul campus, a name-brand fashion or design program, or a business/AI degree without needing full bilingual instruction from day one — provided you're comfortable building Korean proficiency through KLI first. It's a weaker fit for students who need an English-medium degree track immediately or who specifically want a co-ed undergraduate cohort, since undergraduate admission is limited to women while the graduate school remains co-ed.
