## A Gyeonggi Campus 20 Minutes from Gangnam by Subway

Gachon University's Korean Language Institute sits on the Global Campus in Sujeong-gu, Seongnam, one stop past the Seoul city line on Bundang Line rail. Students who want lower rent than Sinchon or Hongdae but still want a same-day commute into central Seoul often shortlist Gachon for exactly this reason: Bokjeong Station is a 10-minute walk from the main gate, and from there it is roughly 35-40 minutes to Gangnam Station without a transfer. Seongnam itself is a self-contained satellite city of over 900,000 people, with the Pangyo tech corridor (Korea's "Silicon Valley") a short bus ride north, so the area also draws Korean-language learners already working in IT who study evenings or weekends.

## Program Structure and Weekly Load

The institute runs six proficiency levels mapped to TOPIK I and TOPIK II bands, with roughly 10 weeks per quarter and four intakes a year (March, June, September, December). Regular-track students attend 20 class hours a week, split across grammar, speaking, listening/writing, and a rotating culture or media-Korean elective. Class size is capped near 15 students to keep speaking time high, and placement is decided by a written test plus a short interview on orientation day rather than by nationality or prior coursework alone.

| Level | TOPIK Target | Weekly Hours | Typical Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 (Beginner) | Pre-TOPIK I | 20 hrs | 2 quarters |
| 3-4 (Intermediate) | TOPIK II 3-4 | 20 hrs | 2 quarters |
| 5-6 (Advanced) | TOPIK II 5-6 | 20 hrs | 2 quarters |

## Tuition and the Costs Students Often Forget

Quarterly tuition runs about KRW 1,650,000, with an KRW 80,000 one-time application/registration fee and roughly KRW 85,000 per quarter for textbooks and workbooks. What catches new students off guard is less the sticker price than the recurring extras: the KRW 60,000-100,000 national health insurance (NHIS) premium billed monthly once a D-4 visa is registered, the Alien Registration Card fee, and a refundable dorm deposit that is separate from the quarterly room fee. Budgeting an extra KRW 150,000-200,000 per quarter beyond tuition is realistic.

## Dormitory Life on a Hillside Campus

Gachon's Global Campus dorms are on-site, which matters more here than at inner-Seoul schools because Sujeong-gu's terrain is hilly and off-campus studios near the subway command a premium. Rooms are typically shared doubles with a shared bathroom per floor, laundry rooms, and a curfew that is enforced more strictly than most students expect coming from home countries with looser dorm norms. Priority for dorm placement usually goes to first-quarter international students and those coming from outside the Seoul metro area, so applying early in the admission window (roughly 3 months before intake) meaningfully improves the odds of a room.

## TOPIK Culture and Why Attendance Is Non-Negotiable

The institute builds TOPIK preparation into the core curriculum rather than treating it as an optional evening class, with mock exams scheduled near the end of each quarter and past-paper drills woven into reading and listening blocks from level 3 onward. This matters for visa purposes as much as academic ones: D-4 language-student visas in Korea are tied to attendance, and immigration expects 90% or higher class attendance for visa extension and for eligibility to apply to a degree program afterward. Gachon's registrar tracks attendance weekly rather than only at quarter-end, so absences are flagged and addressed before they jeopardize a student's status, which is a meaningfully different rhythm from institutes that only review attendance at the finish line.

## Getting There From Incheon Airport and Seoul

From Incheon International Airport, the most common route is the AREX express or a limousine bus to Seoul Station or Gangnam, followed by a Bundang Line or Sinbundang Line connection to Bokjeong or Seongnam Station — a total trip of about 2 to 2.5 hours depending on connections. Students settling in for a full year sometimes instead book an airport taxi directly to the dormitory for the first arrival, given the volume of luggage typical of a long-term move, then rely on the subway for daily commuting afterward. Within Seongnam, campus shuttle buses connect the Global Campus to Bokjeong Station and to the nearby Yatap and Pangyo areas during peak class-start and class-end hours, which is useful for students living in off-campus housing slightly farther from the main gate.

## Community and Campus Life Beyond the Classroom

A buddy program pairs incoming international students with Korean undergraduates for the first quarter, which tends to be the fastest way new arrivals learn practical things no orientation packet covers — which convenience store has the cheapest lunch sets, how the campus gym registration works, or which professors run popular open electives. The institute also runs quarterly cultural excursions (palace visits, a Han River trip, a DMZ or Nami Island day trip depending on the season) that double as informal speaking practice outside the classroom. Combined with Seongnam's relatively low cost of living compared to central Seoul, these programs are a meaningful part of why students who prioritize budget and community over nightlife proximity choose Gachon over a downtown-Seoul institute.
