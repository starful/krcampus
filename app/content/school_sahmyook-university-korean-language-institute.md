## Where Nowon-gu Puts You on the Seoul Map

Sahmyook University's Korean Language Institute (삼육대학교 한국어교육원) sits on the university's main campus at 815 Hwarang-ro, Nowon-gu, in northeastern Seoul. This isn't Hongdae or Sinchon — Nowon-gu is a residential, family-oriented district known for lower living costs, quiet streets, and easy access to Mount Suraksan and Mount Bulamsan. The nearest subway access runs through Seoul Girls' High School Station and Hwarangdae Station on Line 6, with a short bus ride or 15-minute walk to campus. For students who want to avoid Seoul's busiest entertainment districts — while staying roughly 40-50 minutes by subway from Hongdae, Myeongdong, and Gangnam — this location is a deliberate trade-off worth weighing.

## A Seventh-day Adventist Campus Culture

Sahmyook University is Korea's principal Seventh-day Adventist institution, and that identity shapes daily campus life. Chapel or campus devotional activities are part of the university calendar, the cafeteria leans vegetarian-friendly, and Saturday (rather than Sunday) is observed campus-wide as a day of rest. International students of any or no religious background are welcomed into the language program, but those who prefer a fully secular campus should factor this in. In exchange, students often cite a calmer, safety-conscious atmosphere and an international mix of classmates drawn from Adventist partner institutions worldwide.

## Program Structure and Weekly Schedule

The Regular Korean Program runs six proficiency levels, from absolute beginner (Level 1) through advanced (Level 6), each spanning one 10-week quarter. Classes meet Monday through Friday, generally four hours a day, split between grammar-focused morning sessions and speaking/listening practice. Placement testing on orientation day sets the starting level for students with prior Korean study; complete beginners start at Level 1 regardless of intake month.

| Level | Focus | Typical TOPIK Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Hangeul, survival Korean, basic grammar | Pre-TOPIK / TOPIK I prep |
| 3-4 | Intermediate grammar, everyday conversation | TOPIK II (Level 3-4) |
| 5-6 | Academic Korean, essay writing, debate | TOPIK II (Level 5-6) |

Class sizes are capped around 15-18 students per section — smaller than several downtown institutes — allowing more individual speaking time. Afternoons often bring supplementary activities: Korean cooking classes, calligraphy, and field trips to palaces, museums, and traditional markets around Seoul.

## Quarterly Cost Breakdown

Budgeting is straightforward once you separate recurring tuition from one-time entry fees. New students pay a one-time application/registration fee; textbook costs are billed per quarter since materials change with each level.

| Item | Amount (KRW) | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Application/registration fee | 80,000 | One-time, new students only |

Beyond tuition, budget separately for the D-4 visa fee, mandatory health insurance (roughly 30,000-50,000 KRW/month), dormitory or off-campus rent, and daily living costs. Nowon-gu's off-campus rents and meal prices run noticeably lower than Seoul's central districts, partially offsetting the commute time for students who venture downtown often.

## Dormitory Life at Sahmyook Global House

Most first-quarter international students opt for on-campus housing rather than searching for a goshiwon or officetel from abroad. Sahmyook's dormitory (Global House) offers shared double rooms with basic furnishings, on-site laundry, and proximity to the cafeteria and classroom buildings — a real convenience early on. Demand exceeds supply at the start of each quarter, so apply as early as the institute allows, generally right after the admission offer. Students who don't secure a dorm room typically look at officetels and shared housing in Nowon-gu or neighboring Dobong-gu, where deposits and rent run lower than in university-town hotspots like Sinchon or Hyehwa.

## D-4 Visa Steps Specific to This Institute

Admitted students receive a Standard Admission Letter and Certificate of Enrollment from the KLI, the core of the D-4 (language training) visa application at a Korean embassy or consulate. Required documents typically include a bank statement showing sufficient funds (often around USD 10,000-20,000 depending on the consulate), a completed visa application form, passport photos, and proof of highest education completed. Because Sahmyook is religious-affiliated, some consulates may ask clarifying questions about the institution during the visa interview — be ready to explain that religious affiliation doesn't affect academic enrollment or coursework. After arrival, register for the Alien Registration Card (ARC) within 90 days at the Nowon-gu district immigration office or an assigned center, and report any address change tied to dormitory move-out.

## TOPIK Outcomes and Next Steps

The curriculum is explicitly aligned to TOPIK benchmarks at each level, and the institute schedules regular mock exams so students can track readiness before sitting the official test. Graduates of Level 4 or above commonly pursue TOPIK II certification needed for Korean university admission or certain employment visas, and a meaningful share of KLI graduates continue directly into Sahmyook University's undergraduate or graduate programs, where language institute completion can help, though it doesn't guarantee, admission. Students planning to transfer to a different Korean university after finishing the language program should keep transcripts and attendance records from the KLI, since receiving institutions frequently request them as part of the application file.
