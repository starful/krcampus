# Signing a Room Contract in Korea: A Guide for Students

Finding housing is one of the first real tests of independence for international students in Korea. Korea uses distinctive deposit systems — **Wolse** and **Jeonse** — plus licensed real estate agents called **budongsan (부동산)** who broker nearly every private rental. This guide covers how the process works, what to check before signing, and how to protect your deposit.

## Wolse vs. Jeonse: Korea's Two Deposit Systems

Korea's rental market runs on two structures uncommon elsewhere, and understanding them is essential before apartment hunting.

- **Wolse (월세)** — monthly rent. You pay a smaller deposit (bojeungeum, 보증금) plus monthly rent. This is the standard option for students.
- **Jeonse (전세)** — a lump-sum deposit, often 60–80% of the property's value, with **zero monthly rent**. The landlord invests the deposit and returns it in full when you move out. Jeonse requires huge capital (often ₩100–300 million) and is rarely realistic for students without family support.
- **Banjeonse (반전세)** — a hybrid: a mid-sized deposit plus reduced monthly rent, sometimes offered as a negotiation point.

For most students, Wolse is practical. A typical **officetel** or **one-room** near a Seoul university runs a deposit of ₩3–10 million with monthly rent of ₩400,000–800,000, though prices vary by district and building age.

| Deposit Type | Typical Upfront Deposit | Monthly Rent | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wolse (월세) | ₩3,000,000–10,000,000 | ₩400,000–800,000 | Most students, 1-year stays |
| Banjeonse (반전세) | ₩30,000,000–80,000,000 | ₩100,000–300,000 | Longer stays, some savings |
| Jeonse (전세) | ₩100,000,000+ | ₩0 | Families, long-term residents with capital |

## Working With a Budongsan (Licensed Real Estate Agent)

Almost all off-campus rentals are brokered through a **budongsan**, a storefront agency clustered near subway exits and university gates. Agents are government-licensed and legally required to explain the contract and verify ownership before you sign — always use one rather than renting directly from a stranger online.

- **Agent fees (중개수수료)** are usually paid by both tenant and landlord separately, calculated as a percentage of the deposit plus 100x monthly rent (a "conversion" formula standardizing Jeonse-style calculations). For Wolse contracts under ₩50 million converted value, the legal cap is around 0.4–0.5%, but confirm the exact rate since it's regulated locally.
- Ask for the agent's business registration number and check it matches the sign posted in their office.
- A good agent accompanies you to view multiple units, negotiates with the landlord, and files the contract registration.
- Never pay a deposit into an agent's personal account — payment always goes to the landlord's registered account; the agent brokers and verifies, not holds funds.

## Reading the Contract: Key Clauses to Check

Korean lease contracts (임대차계약서) are typically one to two pages on a standard government template, but details matter. Before signing, verify:

1. **Registered owner match** — the landlord's name must match the property registry document (**등기부등본**), which your agent should pull and show you. This reveals existing mortgages or liens — a red flag if the loan plus your deposit exceeds the property's value.
2. **Deposit return clause** — confirm the date and method for your refund at contract's end, and late-payment interest if the landlord misses it.
3. **Special conditions (특약사항)** — this section overrides general clauses. Ensure it states who pays for repairs, whether subletting is allowed, and expected move-out condition.
4. **Maintenance fee (관리비)** — separate from rent, covering upkeep, common electricity, and sometimes internet/water. Get the average monthly amount in writing; it can add ₩50,000–150,000 to your real cost.
5. **Contract duration and early termination penalty** — most leases run 12 months; breaking early usually forfeits part of the deposit or requires finding a replacement tenant.

## Protecting Your Deposit: Confirmation of Fixed Date

The most important legal safeguard for tenants is the **Confirmation of Fixed Date (확정일자)**, obtained at your local **Community Service Center (jumin center)** after signing. This stamps your contract with an official date, giving you legal priority to reclaim your deposit if the landlord's property is later seized or sold.

1. Sign the lease and pay the deposit.
2. Complete **resident registration (전입신고)** at the district office — also required for your Alien Registration Card (ARC) address update.
3. Request the Fixed Date stamp on your contract at the same visit — free and takes minutes.
4. Keep the stamped contract safe for any future dispute.

Students on a D-2 or D-4 visa should update their ARC address within 14 days of moving, since Immigration checks this against your registered residence.

## Dormitory vs. Private Housing: Weighing the Trade-offs

Many students start in university dormitories before moving to private rentals, which affects both budget and paperwork.

| Factor | University Dormitory | Private Rental (Wolse) |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Semester fee, no separate deposit | Deposit ₩3M–10M+ |
| Contract complexity | Simple, university-issued | Full legal lease, agent required |
| Monthly cost | Often included in dorm fee | ₩400,000–800,000 + utilities |
| Flexibility | Fixed semester terms | Negotiable 6–12 month terms |
| Location | On or near campus | Anywhere, subway-dependent |

Dorms are simpler for a first semester, but private housing offers more control over location, roommates, and cost once you're comfortable navigating a lease.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I sign a lease before arriving in Korea?**
It's possible but risky — agents strongly recommend viewing the unit in person, since photos rarely show condition, noise, or mold issues accurately.

**Do I need a Korean guarantor?**
Not usually for standard Wolse contracts, though some landlords ask for one without Korean credit or income history. A stable deposit and clear terms usually suffice.

**What if my landlord refuses to return my deposit?**
With a Fixed Date confirmation, you can file for a **payment order (지급명령)** through the local court, or seek help from the **Housing and Urban Guarantee Corporation (HUG)**, which offers deposit insurance (전세보증금반환보증) worth considering for larger deposits.

**How early should I start looking?**
Begin 4–6 weeks before your semester starts — areas near major universities fill quickly, especially at the start of spring (March) and fall (September) semesters.

Understanding Wolse, Jeonse, and the role of a budongsan turns an intimidating process into a manageable checklist. Review the registry document, secure your Fixed Date stamp right after signing, and you'll have a solid legal foundation for your time in Korea.
