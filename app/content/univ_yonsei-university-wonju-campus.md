## Why Yonsei Chose Wonju for Its Second Campus

Yonsei University opened its Wonju site in 1978 as a satellite campus, but it has since grown into a self-contained academic community with its own colleges, its own admissions track, and its own identity inside the Yonsei system. Since 2021 the university has marketed this location internally as the **Mirae (Future) Campus**, though the legal address and most external references still say Wonju. For prospective students the practical point is this: a degree earned here reads as "Yonsei University" on the diploma, but the campus culture, class sizes, and cost of living are meaningfully different from the flagship Sinchon campus in Seoul. If your goal is a large-city, humanities-heavy Yonsei experience, Sinchon is the better fit. If your goal is health sciences, applied engineering, or a quieter campus with strong lab access, Wonju is worth serious consideration.

## The Hospital on Campus: Wonju's Medical Cluster

What sets this campus apart from most Korean regional universities is Wonju Severance Christian Hospital, a full teaching hospital physically adjacent to campus and staffed in part by Yonsei-affiliated faculty. This gives the College of Health Sciences — Nursing, Physical Therapy, Occupational Therapy, Radiological Science, Dental Hygiene, and Clinical Pathology — direct clinical placement access that many nursing and allied-health programs elsewhere have to arrange through outside partnerships. Wonju itself has grown into a designated "Medical Device Valley," a government-backed cluster of medical device manufacturers and biotech firms that regularly recruit interns and graduates straight from this campus, which matters for anyone weighing post-graduation work options (D-10 job-seeking visa or E-7 employment visa pathways).

## Tuition and Fee Structure

South Korea phased out the separate one-time 입학금 (admission fee) at most universities, including Yonsei, so it no longer appears as a line item for new entrants. Semester tuition is set per college rather than campus-wide, with health sciences and engineering costing more than humanities-style majors due to lab and clinical fees.

| Cost Item | Estimated Amount (KRW) | Notes |

## Getting to and Around Wonju

Wonju sits roughly 130 km east of Seoul in Gangwon Province, reachable by KTX or ITX-Cheongchun trains from Cheongnyangni or Seoul Station in about 50–90 minutes depending on the line, or by intercity bus in around 1.5–2 hours. This makes weekend trips to Seoul realistic without living there, which is one reason the campus draws students who want lower rent but still want Seoul access. Locally, the campus is served by city buses connecting Wonju Station and the KTX Wonju/Manjong station to the Heungeop-myeon campus area; most students without a car rely on these routes plus bicycles for the last stretch, since the immediate campus neighborhood is smaller and more spread out than a dense Seoul district.

## Visa Track and Korean-Language Expectations

International applicants generally enter on a D-2 (student) visa tied to degree enrollment, following the same Korean Immigration Service documentation as any other four-year university: an admission letter, proof of financial capacity (commonly a bank statement equivalent to roughly one year of tuition and living costs), and a completed visa application at a Korean consulate or via the HiKorea system. TOPIK level 3 is a common informal threshold for Korean-medium majors like Nursing and most Health Sciences departments, since clinical terminology and instructor-led coursework are delivered almost entirely in Korean; TOPIK 4+ is safer for students without prior Korean study. English-track admits in Global Business and select Engineering programs face a lighter Korean-language bar at entry but should expect that campus life, part-time work, and clinical or lab environments still run primarily in Korean, so ongoing Korean study is strongly advised regardless of major.

## Daily Costs and Dorm Life in Heungeop-myeon

Wonju's cost of living runs well below Seoul's. A realistic monthly budget outside tuition — dorm or off-campus room, meals, transit, and incidentals — lands in the 700,000–1,000,000 KRW range for students who cook some meals and eat at campus cafeterias regularly, compared to 1,200,000+ KRW commonly cited for central Seoul. On-campus dormitories are the default for first-year and international students and are usually the most cost-effective option given limited off-campus housing density immediately around Heungeop-myeon; students who want to live off-campus typically look toward Wonju's Danonui-dong or areas closer to Wonju Station, accepting a longer commute in exchange for more city amenities.
