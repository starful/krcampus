# Women's Universities in Korea: Ewha, Sookmyung, and Beyond

South Korea is home to several highly ranked women's universities that actively recruit international students, offer English-taught degrees, and sit in the middle of major cities like Seoul. If you're searching for a strong academic reputation, a supportive single-gender campus culture, and solid post-graduation networks (K-beauty, media, diplomacy, and business all have deep alumni ties to these schools), this guide breaks down where to apply, what it costs, and how the visa process works.

## Why Study at a Women's University in Korea

Women's universities in Korea are not a niche option — Ewha Womans University alone enrolls over 20,000 students and is consistently ranked among Asia's top 100 universities. These schools were founded in the late 1800s and early 1900s as some of Korea's first institutions to offer higher education to women, and today they combine that legacy with modern facilities, K-pop-adjacent media programs, and strong corporate recruiting pipelines (Samsung, LG, and major Korean broadcasters recruit heavily from Ewha and Sookmyung). International students choose them for smaller class sizes in humanities and design fields, active mentorship culture, and campuses located in walkable, safe neighborhoods of Seoul.

## Ewha Womans University: Seoul's Flagship

Located in Seodaemun-gu, a 10-minute walk from Sinchon Station (Line 2), Ewha is the largest and most internationally recognized women's university in Korea. It offers over 40 undergraduate majors taught fully in English through the Ewha International program, plus graduate programs in business (Ewha School of Business has AACSB accreditation), international studies, and law. The Ewha Language Center is one of the most popular Korean-language institutes in Seoul, drawing thousands of D-4 visa students each year for its 10-week intensive terms. The campus itself — including the iconic underground "Ewha Campus Complex" — is a well-known Seoul landmark and appears frequently in K-drama filming locations.

## Sookmyung and Other Women's Universities Worth Comparing

Sookmyung Women's University, in Yongsan-gu near Cheongpa-dong, is Ewha's closest peer in size and reputation, known for strong programs in economics, Chinese studies, and its dedicated Global Service major for international students. Beyond these two, several other women's universities welcome international applicants with lower tuition and less competitive admissions:

| University | Location | Known For | Competitiveness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ewha Womans University | Seodaemun-gu, Seoul | Business, Law, Media, K-pop/design fields | Very high |
| Sookmyung Women's University | Yongsan-gu, Seoul | Economics, Chinese Studies, Global Service | High |
| Sungshin Women's University | Seongbuk-gu, Seoul | Fashion, Beauty Design, Culinary Science | Moderate |
| Duksung Women's University | Dobong-gu, Seoul | Pharmacy, Design, IT | Moderate |
| Seoul Women's University | Nowon-gu, Seoul | Social Welfare, Nursing, Christian Studies | Moderate |

Sungshin and Duksung are particularly popular with students targeting Korea's fashion and beauty industries, since both run partnerships with local cosmetics and design companies for internships. Seoul Women's University has one of the country's most established nursing programs and accepts a meaningful number of international transfer students.

## Degree Pathways and Korean-Language Programs

Most international students enter through one of three tracks: a direct English-taught bachelor's degree, a Korean-taught degree requiring TOPIK Level 3–4, or a one-year Korean Language Institute (KLI) program used as a bridge before degree admission. Ewha and Sookmyung both run KLI tracks with 10-week terms (roughly four terms per year), which many students use to build TOPIK scores while adjusting to Seoul before switching to a D-2 student visa for their actual degree.

| Pathway | Typical Duration | Visa Type | Language Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Korean Language Institute (KLI) | 1–2 years (4–8 terms) | D-4 | None to start |
| English-taught Bachelor's | 4 years | D-2 | IELTS 5.5+ / TOEFL 80+ (varies) |
| Korean-taught Bachelor's | 4 years | D-2 | TOPIK 3–4 |
| Graduate (Master's/PhD) | 2–5 years | D-2 | TOPIK 3+ or English proficiency, program-dependent |

Popular English-taught majors at these schools include International Studies, Business Administration, Communication & Media, and Computer Science — Ewha's Computer Science and AI programs have expanded significantly in the past few years to meet demand from both domestic and international applicants.

## Tuition, Living Costs, and Scholarships

Tuition at private women's universities runs comparable to other major Korean private universities — expect to pay more at Ewha and Sookmyung than at national universities, but less than in the US, UK, or Australia.

- **Ewha Womans University**: roughly 4.0–5.5 million KRW per semester (humanities/social sciences), up to 7 million KRW for business or engineering-adjacent majors.
- **Sookmyung Women's University**: approximately 3.5–5 million KRW per semester.
- **Sungshin, Duksung, Seoul Women's University**: generally 3–4.5 million KRW per semester, among the more affordable private options in Seoul.
- **Living costs**: budget 700,000–1,000,000 KRW/month in Seoul for dorm/rent, food, and transport; dorm rooms at Ewha and Sookmyung typically run 1–1.5 million KRW per semester.

Scholarship coverage is a major draw. Ewha's Global Leader Scholarship and Sookmyung's Global Service Scholarship can cover 30–100% of tuition for competitive international applicants, and many schools offer renewable scholarships tied to maintaining a 3.0+ GPA. The Korean government's GKS (Global Korea Scholarship) also partners with several of these universities for full-ride graduate placements — check availability each admission cycle since seats are limited and awarded by home-country quota.

## Visa Steps and Application Timeline

Once admitted, your university issues a Certificate of Admission, which you use to apply for either a D-4 visa (language programs) or D-2 visa (degree programs) at your nearest Korean embassy or consulate. Required documents typically include your passport, admission letter, proof of financial capacity (bank statement showing sufficient funds, often 10,000+ USD equivalent or a bank balance certificate), and a completed visa application form. After entering Korea, register for your Alien Registration Card (ARC) within 90 days at the local immigration office — international student offices at Ewha and Sookmyung both run orientation sessions that walk new students through this in person.

Application cycles generally run twice yearly: apply by **September–October** for Spring admission (March start) or **April–May** for Fall admission (September start). Language program applications typically close 6–8 weeks before each term begins, so start gathering documents at least three months ahead.

## Campus Life, Dorms, and Safety

Single-gender campuses in Korea tend to have a noticeably close-knit, supportive social atmosphere, with strong "sunbae-hoobae" (senior-junior) mentorship traditions that international students are actively invited into. Ewha and Sookmyung both have international student associations that organize welcome events, Korean-language exchange (language partner "school buddy" programs), and cultural trips. Dorms are mixed in quality — Ewha's newer international dorms have private or shared rooms with study lounges, while older buildings use shared bathrooms per floor. Seoul is consistently ranked one of the safest capital cities in the world, and campuses located near subway lines (Sinchon, Ewha Womans University Station, Sookmyung Women's University Station) make late-night travel straightforward and low-risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can male students apply to these universities?** No — undergraduate programs are for women only, though some graduate programs and special exchange programs occasionally admit male students; check each university's graduate school policy directly.

**Do I need TOPIK to apply?** Not for English-taught degrees or KLI programs, but Korean-taught majors generally require TOPIK 3 or higher, and graduate programs often require TOPIK 4+ unless the program is explicitly English-medium.

**Is Ewha harder to get into than Sookmyung?** Generally yes — Ewha's acceptance rate for international applicants is more competitive, particularly for business and media majors, so many students use Sookmyung or Sungshin as a strong alternative with similar Seoul-based opportunities.
