## Where Hannam Fits Among Daejeon's Korean Programs

Daejeon isn't Seoul or Busan, and that's precisely the pitch. 한남대학교 한국어교육원 (Hannam University Korean Language Institute) sits inside a science-and-education city — home to KAIST, the Daedeok Innopolis research cluster, and a large expat researcher population — but keeps living costs noticeably lower than the capital. The institute runs quarterly Korean-language cohorts on Hannam's main campus in Daedeok-gu, about 20 minutes from Daejeon Station and roughly 50 minutes from Seoul by KTX. For applicants weighing a D-4 language-visa program, the calculus here is straightforward: near-Seoul academic quality, provincial-city rent.

## Program Structure and the Six-Level Ladder

The Regular Korean Program follows the standard six-level structure used across most Korean university language centers, split into Beginner (1-2), Intermediate (3-4), and Advanced (5-6) bands. Each level runs roughly 10 weeks, with four quarters starting in March, June, September, and December — meaning a student entering at Level 1 in March can realistically reach Level 4 by the following spring if they pass each quarter's proficiency checkpoint. Class time totals about 20 hours per week, split between grammar/reading in the morning and speaking/listening practice in the afternoon.

## How Placement and Level Testing Actually Works

New students without prior TOPIK results sit a written and oral placement test in the week before orientation. Those holding a TOPIK certificate can submit it in lieu of the written portion, though most institutes — Hannam included — still run a short speaking interview to confirm the placement isn't out of date. Students who fail to reach 70% attendance or fall below a passing grade in a given quarter are held back a level rather than advanced, a policy that matters directly for visa renewal, since D-4 status renewal is tied to continued enrollment and satisfactory academic progress, not just tuition payment.

## Campus Dormitory Versus Off-Campus Daedeok-gu Housing

Hannam's international dormitory gives first-quarter students priority placement, which matters because D-4 visa applications require a confirmed address before the alien registration card (ARC) can be issued. Dorm rooms are shared, furnished, and walkable to class, running about 700,000–900,000 KRW per quarter. Students who prefer off-campus housing will find officetels and one-room apartments near Ojeong-dong and Osu-dong renting for 300,000–450,000 KRW per month plus a deposit — far below comparable studios near Seoul's Sinchon or Hongdae language-institute clusters. The tradeoff is commute: off-campus housing near Daejeon's language institutes is generally easier to navigate on foot or by bus than in Seoul's denser neighborhoods, so the lower rent doesn't come with a serious convenience penalty.

## Visa Timeline Specific to This Institute

Applicants apply for the D-4-1 language-training visa through the Korean embassy or consulate in their home country after receiving Hannam's certificate of admission, generally issued 4–6 weeks before the quarter start. Because Hannam's quarters begin in March, June, September, and December, students should submit visa applications at least two months ahead of the intended start — later applications risk missing the cohort and having to wait a full quarter. Once in Korea, new students must complete ARC registration at the Daejeon immigration office within 90 days; the institute's international office assists with this appointment, which is one practical advantage of choosing a university-run program over a private hagwon-style language school.

## TOPIK Results and What Graduates Do Next

The institute builds TOPIK I and II preparation directly into Levels 3 through 6, with monthly mock exams under real testing conditions rather than treating TOPIK prep as a separate elective. Many Regular Program graduates use their Level 4-6 completion, combined with TOPIK 3-4, to apply directly into Hannam University's undergraduate or graduate departments, since the institute is embedded in the same university rather than an independent language academy — a meaningful shortcut for students whose eventual goal is a Korean degree rather than just conversational fluency.
