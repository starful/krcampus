## How to Use and Claim National Health Insurance in Korea

If you're studying in Korea on a D-2 or D-4 visa, enrolling in **National Health Insurance Service (NHIS)** is mandatory. Since 2021, all international students must join within their first month, and premiums bill automatically whether or not you use a hospital. This guide covers using your NHIS card at a clinic, what's covered, adding private insurance, and filing claims if you paid out of pocket.

## Why Enrollment Isn't a Choice

Korea's health insurance for foreign students runs through the **local NHIS branch**, and universities typically batch-enroll new international students shortly after alien registration is complete. Without an Alien Registration Card (ARC), NHIS can't process you — so booking your ARC appointment within your first two weeks matters. Monthly premiums for 2026 run **₩113,050–120,000**, and unpaid premiums accumulate: NHIS backdates charges to your entry date if you delay, so there's no benefit to waiting.

Coverage starts the day you're registered, and your ARC number doubles as your insurance ID once linked. You don't get a separate physical NHIS card — your ARC or a linked app ("The건강보험") serves as proof of enrollment at hospital front desks.

## Using Your Coverage at a Clinic or Hospital

Visiting a Korean clinic (의원) or hospital (병원) as an NHIS member is simple, though the workflow differs from many home countries.

1. **Register at the front desk** with your ARC — first visits require a short form (이름/name, 생년월일/birthdate, 연락처/phone).
2. **See the doctor.** Most clinics don't require appointments; walk in during hours (typically 9am–6pm, closed Sundays).
3. **Pay the co-payment on the spot.** NHIS covers roughly 70% of standard costs; you pay the remaining 30% at checkout — no reimbursement paperwork for routine visits.
4. **Collect your prescription slip (처방전)** if needed, then take it to any pharmacy (약국), found near every clinic.

For non-emergencies, start at a local clinic rather than a large hospital — Korea's referral system imposes higher co-pays (and sometimes blocks direct access) if you skip straight to a tertiary hospital without a referral letter.

| Facility Type | Typical Co-pay (NHIS-covered) | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Local clinic (의원) | ~30% of cost, often ₩5,000–15,000 total | Colds, minor injuries, prescriptions |
| Pharmacy (약국) | ~30% of medication cost | Filling prescriptions |
| General hospital (병원) | ~40–50% without referral | Specialist consults, minor procedures |
| Tertiary/university hospital | Higher without referral letter | Surgery, complex diagnostics |
| Emergency room (응급실) | Varies, often 50%+ | Emergencies only — expensive otherwise |

## Finding English-Speaking Clinics

Language, not cost, is usually the biggest friction point. Reliable ways to find English-capable care:

- **1339 Medical Consultation Hotline** (dial 120 in Seoul, request the medical interpretation line) connects you to interpreters in real time during a visit.
- **KHIDI (Korea Health Industry Development Institute)** lists "Medical Korea" accredited hospitals with English-speaking staff, concentrated in Seoul (Gangnam, Itaewon), Busan, and Incheon.
- **University international offices** usually keep a shortlist of partner clinics near campus used to treating international students — ask before your first visit.
- **Severance (Yonsei)**, **Samsung Medical Center**, **Seoul National University Hospital**, and **Asan Medical Center** run International Health Care Centers with English-speaking coordinators, at higher cost than local clinics.
- Naver Map, **Danawa Health**, and **1339** apps let you filter for "foreigner-friendly" clinics with English reviews.

Neighborhood clinics near university areas (Sinchon, Hongdae, Anam) are often used to international patients even without perfect English, since nearby pharmacists are used to translating basic instructions.

## What NHIS Does and Doesn't Cover

NHIS covers most standard care but excludes several categories students commonly need — why many universities require or recommend supplemental private insurance.

| Category | NHIS Coverage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| GP visits, prescriptions | ✅ ~70% covered | Routine care |
| Hospitalization | ✅ ~80% covered | Co-pay caps apply for long stays |
| Dental (basic) | ✅ Partial (scaling, cavities) | Cosmetic/orthodontic excluded |
| Mental health counseling | ⚠️ Limited | Psychiatric visits partly covered; therapy often not |
| Vision (glasses, contacts) | ❌ Not covered | Pay out of pocket |
| Pre-existing conditions | ⚠️ Case-by-case | May require waiting periods |
| Repatriation/emergency evacuation | ❌ Not covered | Requires private travel/medical insurance |
| COVID/flu testing | ✅ Mostly covered | Some rapid tests still out-of-pocket |

Because of these gaps, students on scholarships (like GKS) or at universities with mandatory group plans often carry **dual coverage** — NHIS as primary payer, plus a private supplemental policy (DB Insurance, Samsung Fire & Marine, or Cigna Global) covering the 30% co-pay, dental extras, or evacuation.

## Claiming Reimbursement and Filing Additional Insurance

If you paid full price upfront — common at ERs, out-of-network clinics, or before NHIS registration finished — you can claim reimbursement retroactively.

**For NHIS retroactive claims:**
1. Keep the original receipt (영수증) and detailed statement (진료비 세부내역서) from the hospital.
2. Visit or mail documents to your local NHIS branch (find yours via nhis.or.kr or 1577-1000).
3. Submit the **의료비 지급 청구서** (medical expense claim form), bankbook copy, and ARC copy.
4. Processing takes 2–4 weeks, refunded to your Korean bank account.

**For private insurance claims:**
1. Request an itemized receipt and diagnosis certificate (진단서) at checkout — reissuing later costs extra and takes time.
2. Log into your insurer's portal or app (most offer English UI for student plans) and upload receipts, diagnosis, and policy number.
3. Outpatient claims usually resolve in 1–2 weeks; hospitalization claims take longer.
4. If your university brokered the policy (common for GKS scholars), the international office can often mediate delays — ask before escalating to the insurer.

Keep digital copies of every receipt and diagnosis certificate; Korean clinics don't always resend documents, and a lost 영수증 is the most common cause of delayed claims.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need private insurance if I already have NHIS?**
Not legally, but strongly recommended. NHIS leaves gaps in dental, vision, mental health therapy, and medical evacuation that matter over a multi-year stay.

**What happens if I skip NHIS enrollment?**
You'll be auto-enrolled and billed retroactively from your entry date once the university or immigration flags the gap — there's no opting out as a registered international student.

**Can I use NHIS before receiving my physical ARC?**
No — enrollment requires a valid ARC number. Prioritize your immigration office appointment in your first two weeks.

**Are premiums cheaper for students than for workers?**
Yes, students pay a flat regional average rate (not income-based), usually lower than employed foreign workers' premiums.

**Does NHIS cover treatment back home during university breaks?**
No — NHIS only covers care received in Korea. If traveling, add short-term travel insurance separately.
