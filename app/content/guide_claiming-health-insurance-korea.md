# How to Use and Claim National Health Insurance in Korea

If you're studying in South Korea on a D-2, D-4, or similar student visa, enrollment in the **National Health Insurance Service (NHIS)** is mandatory. Since March 2021, international students staying longer than six months are automatically enrolled after alien registration, usually within the first month of arrival. This guide covers how to use your NHIS card at the doctor, what's covered, how to find English-speaking clinics, and how to claim reimbursements from supplemental private insurance.

## Why Enrollment Is Automatic and What It Costs

Once you register your Alien Registration Card (ARC) at the immigration office, NHIS syncs with immigration data and enrolls you automatically — no separate signup needed. You **do** need to pay premiums promptly, since unpaid premiums can block visa extensions.

As of 2026, international students pay a flat monthly premium set annually by NHIS (around ₩109,000/month in 2025, adjusted yearly). Some students qualify for lower rates as a dependent under a Korean guardian, but most solo students pay the standard rate.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Enrollment trigger | ARC registration (within 90 days of entry) |
| Monthly premium (2026, approx.) | ₩110,000–120,000 (flat rate, reviewed annually) |
| Payment method | Bank auto-debit, Korean bank app, or convenience store (GiroCard) |
| Grace period before penalty | 2 months unpaid → visa extension risk |
| Coverage start | Retroactive to entry date once enrolled |

Premiums are billed monthly via a *gojisu* (고지서) notice sent to your address or linked KakaoTalk. Many universities handle the first payment cycle for freshmen — ask your school's international office if unsure.

## Using Your NHIS Card at the Clinic

Korea no longer issues a physical insurance card by default — your ARC number **is** your insurance ID. At a clinic (의원) or hospital (병원):

1. Present your ARC and say you're an NHIS subscriber ("건강보험 가입자예요").
2. The clinic verifies your status electronically via the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service (HIRA).
3. You pay only the co-payment (본인부담금) at checkout — NHIS covers the rest automatically, no reimbursement forms needed.

For outpatient visits, students typically pay 30–60% of the total cost depending on clinic size. A basic cold consultation might run ₩5,000–15,000 out of pocket, while a specialist visit at a general hospital could run ₩20,000–40,000.

### Prescriptions: The Two-Step Process

Korea separates diagnosis from dispensing. After seeing a doctor, you'll get a prescription slip (처방전) instead of medicine on the spot. Take it to any licensed pharmacy (약국) — usually within a block of clinics — where NHIS coverage applies the same way. Show your ARC and pay the discounted co-pay, often just ₩2,000–5,000. Prescriptions are typically valid for 3 days from issue, so fill them promptly.

## Finding English-Speaking Clinics and Hospitals

This is where most international students struggle, especially outside Seoul. Reliable channels:

- **1339 → 120 Dasan Call Center**: Seoul's multilingual helpline directs you to nearby English-friendly clinics.
- **University health centers**: Most campuses (Yonsei, Korea University, SNU, Ewha, KAIST) have on-site clinics used to international students, often the fastest first stop.
- **Designated "Global Healthcare" hospitals**: Severance Hospital (Yonsei), Samsung Medical Center, Asan Medical Center, and Seoul National University Hospital run International Clinics with English-speaking coordinators, though costs run higher.
- **Naver Map filters**: Search "외국인 진료" (foreigner-friendly treatment) to filter listings with English reviews.

| City/Region | English-Friendly Option | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Seoul | Severance International Health Care Center | Full English intake, higher fees |
| Seoul | Samsung Medical Center | Strong for specialist referrals |
| Busan | Pusan National University Hospital Int'l Clinic | Good for southern-region students |
| Daejeon | KAIST-affiliated campus clinic | Best for KAIST/UST students |
| Any city | University health center | Cheapest, fastest, often bilingual staff |

Smaller regional cities may lack dedicated English clinics — bring a Korean-speaking classmate or use a translation app like Papago, which handles medical vocabulary reasonably well.

## Emergency Care and the 119 System

For emergencies, dial **119** (free, some English support available). NHIS covers ER visits, but co-pays are higher than outpatient care — often 50–60% of costs unless admitted. If hospitalized, NHIS covers a significant share of inpatient costs, but private rooms, non-covered procedures (elective treatments, cosmetic work, some dental), and translator fees are excluded. This is the gap private supplemental insurance is designed to fill.

## Claiming Benefits from Supplemental Private Insurance

Many universities require or recommend a secondary private policy (from DB Insurance, Samsung Fire & Marine, or Cigna Global) to cover what NHIS doesn't — trip cancellation, private rooms, dental, and repatriation. Claiming reimbursement generally follows this sequence:

1. **Pay upfront** at the clinic or hospital as usual (NHIS discount already applied).
2. **Request itemized receipts**: the "진료비 계산서·영수증" (medical fee statement) and "진료확인서" (treatment certificate) — pharmacies also issue itemized drug receipts.
3. **Submit within the claim window**: most policies require submission within 30–90 days of treatment.
4. **Upload via the insurer's app or portal**: scan receipts and prescription details; some insurers accept English translations, others require a Korean original plus your own translation.
5. **Track reimbursement**: typical turnaround is 1–3 weeks for outpatient claims, longer for hospitalization.

Keep every receipt — Korean clinics generally issue them by default, but confirm before leaving, since reprints can take days to arrange.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I opt out of NHIS if I already have private insurance from home?**
Generally no — NHIS enrollment is mandatory for stays over six months regardless of existing foreign coverage, per 2021 immigration-linked reforms.

**What happens if I miss a premium payment?**
Unpaid premiums accumulate and can flag your account during ARC renewal or visa extension, so pay promptly even if you rarely use healthcare services.

**Does NHIS cover dental and vision?**
Partially — basic scaling and some fillings are covered, but orthodontics, implants, and most vision correction (glasses, LASIK) are not; this is a common reason students add private supplemental coverage.

**Can I use NHIS immediately after landing in Korea?**
Coverage activates once your ARC is issued and NHIS enrollment syncs, typically within 2–4 weeks of arrival — retroactive to your entry date, so early medical costs can sometimes be reclaimed once enrolled.

Understanding this system early — rather than during a medical emergency — saves money and stress. Register your ARC promptly, keep premium payments current, and identify your nearest English-friendly clinic before you need one.
