## A Two-Campus National University on Korea's East Coast

국립강릉원주대학교 (Gangneung-Wonju National University, GWNU) is a national university formed in 2007 through the merger of Gangneung National University, founded in 1946, and Wonju National University. Rather than one campus, GWNU operates two: the **Gangneung campus** on Korea's East Sea coast in Gangwon State, and the **Wonju campus** roughly 100 km inland. This matters more than it sounds — your major decides which city becomes your home for four years, so international applicants should confirm campus location before submitting documents, not after arrival. Gangneung is the larger, older campus and hosts most liberal arts, science, engineering, and the university's flagship dental college. Wonju is smaller and centers on pharmacy and health sciences.

## Academic Colleges and What Each Campus Specializes In

GWNU's academic strength is split cleanly by campus. Gangneung hosts the College of Dentistry — one of only 11 dental schools nationwide and the sole dental college in Gangwon Province — alongside the College of Marine Sciences, which draws on direct East Sea access for oceanography, fisheries science, and marine biotechnology. Humanities, natural sciences, engineering, business, and arts & physical education are also based in Gangneung. Wonju is anchored by the College of Pharmacy and College of Health Science, along with programs in human ecology and social welfare.

## Tuition and Cost of Living in Gangwon

As a national university, GWNU's tuition runs well below private universities in Seoul, and — like other national universities — the separate 입학금 (admission fee) has been phased out since 2021, so incoming students are not charged one. Yearly tuition still varies by college, with engineering, dentistry, and pharmacy priced highest due to lab and equipment fees.

## How International Students Apply

GWNU's International Office runs a dedicated 외국인 전형 (foreigner admission track) separate from the regular Korean-student exam-based process, with intakes for both the March and September semesters. Required documents typically include a notarized diploma, academic transcripts, a passport copy, a financial statement (proof of at least USD 20,000 or equivalent, often via bank balance certificate), a health/TB check, and a Korean or English proficiency certificate depending on the program's teaching language. Most Korean-taught majors ask for TOPIK level 3 or higher at admission, with level 4 commonly required by graduation for degree conferral; English-taught tracks are limited and concentrated in a handful of graduate programs, so most undergraduates should plan on reaching working Korean proficiency before enrolling.

## Visa, Residency, and Health Insurance Steps

Admitted degree-seeking students apply for a **D-2 visa** at a Korean embassy or consulate using GWNU's certificate of admission (표준입학허가서) and the standard visa document set. After arrival, register at the Gangneung or Wonju immigration office for an Alien Registration Card (ARC) within 90 days — this card is required to open a Korean bank account, sign a lease, and get a phone plan. Enrollment in Korea's National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) is mandatory for all international students holding a D-2 or D-4 visa, with monthly premiums currently in the range of 60,000–110,000 KRW depending on income bracket and any dependents.

## Living in Gangneung: Beaches, Coffee, and the KTX Line

Gangneung is a mid-sized coastal city best known nationally for Gyeongpo Beach and the Anmok Coffee Street, a cluster of dozens of independent cafés along the shore — a genuine local perk for students used to landlocked campus life. The KTX Gangneung Line connects the city to Seoul's Cheongnyangni Station in roughly two hours, making weekend trips to the capital realistic without a car. Wonju, by contrast, sits closer to the middle of the country along the Yeongdong Expressway and has faster road access to both Seoul and Chuncheon, though slightly less scenic surroundings. Both cities are considerably cheaper than Seoul for rent and daily expenses, which is part of why GWNU is often recommended to budget-conscious applicants who still want a national-university degree and a recognized professional program like dentistry or pharmacy.

## Dormitories and On-Campus Housing

Both campuses operate dormitories, and international students are generally prioritized for at least their first year, since off-campus housing in Gangneung and Wonju is less internationally-oriented than in Seoul (fewer English-speaking landlords, less short-term lease flexibility). Dorm fees run roughly 500,000–900,000 KRW per semester depending on room type (shared vs. single) and meal plan inclusion — check current rates with the housing office, as they adjust yearly. Students who miss the dorm application window should start apartment searches early, ideally with help from the International Office or a Korean-speaking senior student, since deposit and lease conventions in Gangwon differ from Seoul's more foreigner-familiar rental market.
