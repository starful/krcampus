Silla University's Korean Language Institute (신라대학교 한국어교육원) sits on the university's main hilltop campus in Sasang-gu, on Busan's western side, and runs a 10-week, six-level program that starts four times a year. It is one of the more affordable language institutes in a metropolitan city, which is the main reason it draws budget-conscious students from Vietnam, China, Uzbekistan, and Japan who want a D-4 visa pathway without Seoul-level living costs.

## Why Sasang-gu Instead of Downtown Busan

The campus is not in the touristy Haeundae or Nampo-dong areas — it's in Sasang-gu, a residential and light-industrial district connected to central Busan by Busan Subway Line 2. That means rent, food, and daily transport run noticeably cheaper than at institutes near Seomyeon or Busan National University. A round trip to Nampo-dong or Gwangalli Beach takes about 40–50 minutes by subway, which students typically do on weekends rather than daily. For anyone comparing total cost of a year in Korea rather than just tuition, this location shift is the single biggest saving.

## Term Structure and Tuition Breakdown

The academic year is split into four 10-week terms (spring, summer, fall, winter), each covering one proficiency sub-level. Most new students enter in March or September, though June and December intakes exist for those already holding a visa or transferring from another D-4 program.

| Item | Amount (KRW) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Application fee | 80,000 | Non-refundable, paid with documents |
| Quarterly tuition | 1,650,000 | Per 10-week term |
| Textbook/material fee | 80,000 | Per term, institute-published + Sogang/Yonsei series |
| Approx. total per term | 1,730,000 | Tuition + textbooks, excludes housing |

On top of this, budget roughly 500,000–650,000 KRW per term for a dormitory room and meals if you don't cook, or less if you self-cater in a shared officetel near campus.

## Level Structure and Weekly Class Load

Classes run Monday–Friday, generally 4 hours a day (20 hours per week), split between grammar/reading in the morning and speaking/listening practice in smaller groups in the afternoon. Class size is capped around 15 students to keep speaking time high, which matters more at a mid-sized regional institute than at large Seoul programs where sections can run 20+.

| Level | Focus | Typical TOPIK Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 (Beginner) | Hangeul, survival grammar, daily conversation | Pre-TOPIK |
| 3–4 (Intermediate) | Connective grammar, essay basics, presentations | TOPIK II, Level 3 |
| 5–6 (Advanced) | Academic writing, debate, news/media Korean | TOPIK II, Level 4–5 |

Students aiming for direct undergraduate or graduate admission to Silla University itself get a practical advantage: language institute completion certificates and in-house TOPIK mock scores are recognized directly by the university's international admissions office, which shortens the internal transfer process compared with applying from an unaffiliated institute.

## D-4 Visa Handling for This Institute

Silla University's international office issues the standard document package — certificate of enrollment, standardized application form, and the institute's own guarantee letter — needed for a D-4-1 visa application at a Korean embassy or consulate. Requirements mirror national guidelines: proof of funds (a bank balance certificate showing roughly 10,000 USD equivalent, held for the trailing period the embassy specifies), a completed health check for TB screening on countries subject to the requirement, and passport-sized photos meeting ICAO specs. Because Sasang-gu is not a metropolitan hub like Gangnam or Jongno, visa extension appointments at the Busan Immigration Office tend to have shorter wait times than in Seoul, which students consistently mention as a quality-of-life plus once they're already enrolled.

## Dormitory Options and Off-Campus Alternatives

The on-campus dormitory has roughly 300 beds shared between international and domestic students, mostly double rooms with a shared bathroom per floor and a cafeteria downstairs. Because Sasang-gu itself is lower-cost, many second-term-and-later students move into private officetels a 10–15 minute walk from the gate, where a studio runs 350,000–450,000 KRW/month plus utilities — noticeably less than equivalent options near Seoul's major institutes.

## TOPIK Support and What Happens After Level 6

The institute runs a dedicated TOPIK prep track in the final two weeks of each term, with mock exams scored against the actual TOPIK rubric rather than internal tests only. Most students finishing Level 6 sit for TOPIK II and land in the 3–4 range on a first attempt, with repeat test-takers pushing into Level 5. Graduates commonly move into Silla University's undergraduate programs in business, tourism, or design, transfer to another Busan-area university, or return home with a certificate that satisfies employment-visa Korean-language requirements at Korean firms operating in their home country.

## Who Tends to Choose This Program

Because of the cost structure and Busan location, the student mix skews toward those prioritizing total budget over metropolitan campus life, those with existing family or community ties in Busan (a common pattern for Vietnamese and Uzbek cohorts), and students who plan to continue directly into a Silla University degree program rather than transfer to a Seoul university afterward.
