국립목포대학교 (Mokpo National University) is a national university whose main campus is not actually in Mokpo city — it sits about 20 minutes away in Cheonggye-myeon, Muan-gun, next to Muan International Airport. If you're comparing this school to others in Jeonnam, the two things that set it apart are its founding strength in fisheries/ocean science and its national-university fee structure, both covered below along with tuition, TOPIK requirements, and dorm logistics.

## A Campus in Muan-gun, Not Downtown Mokpo

The university was founded in Mokpo city in 1946 as a teacher-training college and moved to its present 66-hectare Muan-gun site in 1988 as it merged with the former National Fisheries College. It kept the "Mokpo" name for brand continuity, but international applicants who book housing in downtown Mokpo often discover they need a 30-40 minute commute by intercity bus. The airport-adjacent location does have upsides: Muan International Airport runs domestic routes and a growing set of international charters, and the Jeollanam-do provincial government complex (Namak New Town) is a short drive away, which means part-time and internship opportunities in provincial administration are more accessible than the campus's rural setting might suggest.

## Fisheries and Ocean Science as the Institutional Backbone

Unlike most comprehensive national universities, Mokpo National University runs a full College of Fisheries and Ocean Science, a legacy of its 1988 merger with the National Fisheries College. Departments here — including Marine Biology, Fisheries Science, and Marine Technology — give students access to research vessels and aquaculture facilities that are rare among Korea's regional universities. This is the program international graduate applicants most often cite as the reason for choosing Mokpo over larger schools in Gwangju, since West Sea (Yellow Sea) coastal access and government fisheries-agency partnerships are difficult to replicate inland.

## Tuition and Fees by College (2026, Estimated)

As a national university, tuition is set by government guidelines and is roughly 40-50% lower than private universities in the same province. There is no mandatory admission fee (입학금), which national universities phased out by 2022. Figures below are per-semester estimates in KRW; engineering and fisheries/marine tracks run higher due to lab and lab-vessel fees.

## Colleges and Representative Departments

The university is organized into nine undergraduate colleges plus a graduate school. A sample of departments international applicants ask about most:

| College | Representative Departments |
|---|---|
| Engineering | Mechanical Eng., Electronic Eng., Chemical Eng., Architecture |
| Fisheries and Ocean Science | Marine Biology, Fisheries Science, Marine Technology |
| Agriculture and Life Sciences | Horticulture, Animal Science, Food Science |
| Business Administration and Economics | Business Administration, International Trade, Economics |
| Humanities | Korean Language and Literature, English Language and Literature, History |
| Education | Elementary Education, Early Childhood Education |

Korean-language instruction is standard across all departments; a small number of graduate lab tracks in engineering and marine science offer partial English instruction, but applicants should not assume a fully English-taught undergraduate program exists here.

## TOPIK Levels and the D-2 Visa Timeline

Most undergraduate departments require TOPIK level 3 for direct admission, with level 4 preferred for competitive majors like Business Administration; students below TOPIK 3 are typically routed through the university's Korean Language Education Center for one to two semesters first. Once an admission offer is issued, the standard D-2 (student) visa process applies: apply for the Certificate of Admission from the university, submit it with a Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance (CCVI) or apply directly at a Korean consulate, and budget 4-6 weeks for processing before the semester start. Because Muan-gun has no consulate of its own, most applicants outside Korea process the visa through their home-country Korean embassy rather than locally.

## Dormitory Life and the Rural Campus Trade-off

On-campus dormitories are the default choice for international students, since Muan-gun has limited private rental stock compared to a large city. Rooms are typically double-occupancy with per-semester fees far below Seoul-area private housing, but the trade-off is that daily life — groceries, cafes, part-time work — is concentrated in a smaller commercial strip near campus rather than a full city downtown. Students who want city amenities generally use the intercity bus to Mokpo city center or, for a bigger selection, to Gwangju.

## Reaching Mokpo Station and Gwangju

Direct campus buses connect to Mokpo Station, which has KTX and SRT service to Seoul (roughly 2.5-3 hours) and connections south along the Honam line. Gwangju, the nearest major metropolitan hub with more part-time job options and a larger expatriate community, is about an hour away by bus. Muan International Airport being minutes from campus is a genuine convenience for students flying home during breaks, even though its route network is smaller than Incheon's.

## International Student Numbers and Support

Mokpo National University's international student population is smaller than at large Seoul or Busan universities — figures in the low hundreds are typical, split between degree-seeking students (concentrated in fisheries science, business, and Korean-language-adjacent humanities programs) and exchange students. The smaller cohort means more direct access to international-student advisors in the Office of International Affairs, but also a thinner peer network than students find at bigger schools; prospective applicants weighing this school against Chonnam National University in Gwangju should factor that trade-off in alongside the tuition savings and the fisheries-science specialization unique to Mokpo National University.
