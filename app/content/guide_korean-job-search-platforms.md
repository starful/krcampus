# Best Job Search Websites and Apps in Korea for Foreigners

Job hunting in Korea as an international student or recent graduate can feel harder than the job itself. Most listings are in Korean, many portals assume a resident registration number, and visa sponsorship isn't always advertised. This guide covers the platforms that actually work for foreigners — from Korea's biggest domestic portals to English-friendly niche apps — so you spend less time guessing and more time applying.

## Why Job Hunting in Korea Is Different for Foreigners

Korean companies overwhelmingly post openings on domestic portals, not LinkedIn. Saramin and JobKorea — both Korean-only — dominate with hundreds of thousands of listings, but neither filters well for visa sponsorship. Your work eligibility also depends on visa type: D-2 (student) holders can only work part-time with university and immigration permission, while D-10 (job-seeking) and E-7 (specific activity/professional) visas are what most graduates need for full-time work. Knowing this before you open a single app saves weeks of misdirected effort.

## The Big Three Domestic Portals

These sites post the largest volume of jobs in Korea. They're not built for foreigners, but skipping them means missing 80%+ of the market.

- **Saramin (사람인)** — Korea's largest portal, strong for corporate, IT, and manufacturing roles nationwide. You can filter for "외국인 채용" (foreigner hiring), but most postings are Korean-only.
- **JobKorea (잡코리아)** — Similar scale to Saramin, with more startups and SMEs. Useful for checking salary bands (연봉), since many listings disclose pay ranges.
- **Wanted (원티드)** — Korea's most modern portal, popular with startups, tech, and design roles. Cleaner UI, some English postings, and a referral-based system that can boost response rates.

## English-Friendly and Foreigner-Focused Platforms

These platforms exist specifically to bridge Korean employers and non-Korean job seekers.

| Platform | Best For | Language | Visa Sponsorship Info |
|---|---|---|---|
| PeopleNJob | Trading companies, manufacturing, general foreigner-friendly roles | English/Korean | Often listed explicitly |
| Work in Korea (WorkNet-linked) | Government-affiliated jobs, E-7/E-9 visa roles | English/Korean | Yes, government-vetted |
| Korea IT Times / KOTRA job boards | Tech, engineering, foreign-invested firms | English | Sometimes |
| LinkedIn Korea | Multinationals, foreign HQ companies, networking | English | Rarely stated, ask directly |
| Indeed Korea | Aggregated listings from multiple portals | Mixed | Inconsistent, verify per posting |

PeopleNJob is worth bookmarking early — it's one of few Korean portals built for non-native speakers, and many listings state upfront whether the employer handles E-7 sponsorship.

## University Career Centers and Government Programs

Don't underestimate your own campus. Universities with large international populations (Yonsei, Korea University, SKKU, Hanyang, KAIST) run career centers with foreign student advisors who already have relationships with visa-sponsoring employers. Ask specifically about:

- **KOTRA Contact Korea** — connects skilled foreign talent with Korean companies seeking global hires, often for E-7 roles.
- **HiKorea job-seeker visa (D-10)** — lets you stay up to 6 months (renewable, 2 years max) after graduation to search for work without a job offer first.
- **World Job Fair for Foreigners** — an annual government-backed event (usually spring, Seoul) connecting foreign graduates with hiring companies.
- **HRD Korea / WorkNet (워크넷)** — the national employment service site, with a foreigner-specific section tied to E-9 and some E-7 postings.

## Networking Apps and Communities That Actually Get Results

In Korea, *inmaek* (인맥, personal network) still drives much hiring, especially at small and mid-sized firms. Pure job-board applications often get buried; a warm introduction rarely does.

- **LinkedIn** — Still the top tool for reaching HR at multinational and foreign-invested companies (Samsung Electronics' global divisions, LG, foreign banks, tech subsidiaries). Keep your profile in English, but add a short Korean summary line.
- **KakaoTalk open chats and Facebook groups** — Groups like "Foreigners Job Seekers in Korea" or university alumni groups often share leads before public posting.
- **Meetup and industry Slack/Discord communities** — Especially active in Seoul's startup and tech scene (Gangnam, Pangyo), where hiring managers post roles directly.
- **JobPlanet (잡플래닛)** — A Glassdoor-equivalent for researching Korean company culture and salary reviews before applying — critical since foreign employees report widely varying experiences by company.

## Comparing Application Effort vs. Payoff

| Platform | Application Difficulty | Foreigner-Friendliness | Typical Response Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saramin / JobKorea | High (Korean resume, KR-format required) | Low–Medium | 2–4 weeks |
| Wanted | Medium (English CVs sometimes accepted) | Medium | 1–2 weeks |
| PeopleNJob | Low–Medium | High | 1–3 weeks |
| LinkedIn | Low (existing profile reusable) | Medium–High | Variable, often slower |
| University career center referrals | Low (introduction-based) | High | Fastest, often days |

## Building a Korea-Ready Application

Most Korean employers expect a standardized resume (이력서) and cover letter (자기소개서), even for English-speaking roles at foreign firms. Key differences from a Western resume:

1. **Photo included** — Standard on Korean-format resumes, though increasingly optional at startups and multinationals.
2. **Structured personal info** — Date of birth, and sometimes visa status, are commonly requested.
3. **Self-introduction essay (자소서)** — Often required separately, answering set prompts about motivation, strengths, and career goals — closer to a personal statement than a cover letter.
4. **TOPIK score** — At Korean-majority companies, listing your TOPIK level (aim for Level 4+ for office roles, 5–6 for client-facing or management-track roles) improves callback rates, even if not explicitly required.

## Quick FAQ

**Do I need a job offer before applying for a D-10 visa?**
No — the D-10 job-seeking visa is for graduates actively searching, valid up to 6 months per renewal (2 years max).

**Can D-2 student visa holders work part-time while studying?**
Yes, with prior permission from your university and local immigration office, generally capped at 20–25 hours/week during term (unlimited during official vacation periods, with permission).

**Which portal should I start with as a total beginner?**
Start with PeopleNJob and your university career center for foreigner-friendly listings, then add Saramin/JobKorea (with a translation tool) once comfortable with Korean-language postings.

**Is fluent Korean required to get hired?**
Not always — foreign-invested companies, English-medium roles, and some IT positions hire at TOPIK 3–4 or even conversational level — but higher proficiency significantly widens your options across sectors.

Start broad across two or three platforms, but focus real effort on your university's career office and PeopleNJob first — they're built for exactly your situation, and that focus tends to pay off faster than casting a wide net across Korean-only mega-portals alone.
