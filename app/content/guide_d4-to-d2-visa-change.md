# D-4 to D-2 Visa Change in Korea: Step-by-Step Process & Requirements

If you're finishing a Korean language program on a D-4 visa and have been accepted into a bachelor's, master's, or PhD program, you must convert your status to D-2 before classes start. This happens entirely inside Korea at your local immigration office (출입국관리사무소) — no need to fly home or reapply at a consulate. It typically takes 1–3 weeks once your documents are complete.

## Why D-4 Holders Must Change Status Before Enrolling

Your D-4 visa is tied to language study, not degree programs. Once admitted, you must switch to D-2 before your first semester — you cannot attend university classes on a D-4 visa, even informally. Universities won't issue your student ID or allow dorm check-in without proof of D-2 status or an active application receipt. Missing this step is a common reason students lose housing deposits or miss orientation.

## Required Documents Checklist

Requirements vary slightly by office (Seoul vs. regional), but the core set follows Korea's Immigration Office guidelines. Prepare originals plus one photocopy of each.

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Application form (visa change) | Available at the immigration office or HiKorea website |
| Passport + Alien Registration Card (ARC) | Must be valid; original ARC required |
| Certificate of admission (입학허가서) | Issued by the university's international office |
| Certificate of enrollment or student ID proof | Some schools issue this after tuition payment |
| Proof of tuition payment | Bank receipt or official university invoice stamp |
| Financial proof | Bank balance certificate, usually ₩20,000,000+ (varies by school) |
| Health/medical certificate | Required by some universities; TB test sometimes requested |
| Standard Admission Package Checklist (표준입학허가서) | Required for first-time degree-seeking students since 2021 |
| 1 passport-size photo | Taken within last 6 months |
| Visa change fee | ₩60,000, paid by card or revenue stamp |

One overlooked item: the **Standard Admission Package (SAP)**, introduced by the Ministry of Justice to curb visa fraud. Your university submits this digitally, but request a copy for your records — officers sometimes ask to see it during the interview.

## Processing Timeline: From Application to Approval

An in-country status change is usually faster than applying from abroad (2–4 weeks at a consulate), but timing still matters for enrollment deadlines.

| Stage | Typical Duration |
|---|---|
| Document preparation (university side) | 1–2 weeks before your appointment |
| HiKorea online appointment booking | Book 2–4 weeks in advance; slots fill fast near semester start |
| Immigration office review | 1–2 weeks (up to 3 during peak season) |
| ARC reissue (new card with D-2 status) | Same day or mailed within 1 week |
| **Total recommended buffer** | **4–6 weeks before semester start** |

Peak season is late January–February (spring) and July–August (fall), when many D-4 students convert at once. Book your HiKorea appointment as soon as you get your admission letter — don't wait for the tuition deadline, as slots fill fast.

## Submitting the Application: HiKorea and In-Person Steps

1. **Create or log into HiKorea** (hikorea.go.kr) and book a "Status Change" (체류자격변경) appointment at the office covering your current address, not your new university's district.
2. **Upload or bring** all required documents; many offices accept digital pre-upload to speed up your visit.
3. **Attend your appointment** with your ARC and passport — an officer verifies your admission letter against the SAP data from your university.
4. **Pay the ₩60,000 fee** at the kiosk or counter.
5. **Receive a receipt** if approval is pending; it serves as temporary proof of legal status while your new ARC is printed.
6. **Collect your new ARC** showing D-2 status, in person or by registered mail.

Keep your old D-4 ARC until the new card is active — some offices ask you to surrender it at pickup.

## Common Reasons Applications Get Rejected or Delayed

- **Insufficient financial proof** — balances below the threshold, or deposited too recently (offices often want 30+ days of history).
- **Attendance gaps during D-4** — attendance below 90% can trigger questions about your study record.
- **Incomplete SAP** — missing signatures or mismatched details across your passport, ARC, and admission letter.
- **Tuition not fully paid** — most schools require at least a deposit before issuing the enrollment certificate immigration needs.
- **Expired visa status** — if your D-4 expires before the change completes, you must also apply for an extension, adding complexity and sometimes a penalty fee.
- **Program mismatch** — switching to an unrelated program (e.g., a sudden vocational track) can trigger extra scrutiny.

If rejected, you can usually appeal or resubmit within a set window (often 7 days). Contact your international office immediately — they liaise directly with immigration on bulk cases.

## Tips From Experienced Transfer Students

- **Talk to your international office early** — staff often pre-screen documents before your appointment, catching errors in advance.
- **Don't let your D-4 lapse** — apply at least 2 weeks before it expires to avoid a separate extension.
- **Bring extra copies** — officers sometimes request more photocopies on the spot; 2 sets of everything avoids a second trip.
- **Check your ARC address** — if you moved during language school, update it on HiKorea first, since mismatches cause delays.
- **Save digital copies** — scan every document into a cloud folder; you'll need them again for scholarship or work permit applications.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I start classes while my visa change is still processing?**
Most universities allow provisional attendance with an appointment confirmation or receipt, but full enrollment and dorm access often require the completed ARC — confirm your school's policy.

**Do I need a new health check for D-2 even if I did one for D-4?**
Often yes — some universities require a fresh TB/health screening for dorm admission, separate from your D-4 check.

**Is the process different for graduate programs vs. bachelor's programs?**
The steps are the same, but graduate admission letters can arrive later, so apply as soon as your acceptance is confirmed rather than waiting for scholarship notifications.

**What happens if I decide not to enroll after getting D-2?**
You must report your withdrawal to immigration and your university; failing to do so can affect future visa applications in Korea.

Converting from D-4 to D-2 is a well-worn path for thousands of international students each year — staying organized with documents and booking your HiKorea slot early matter most for a smooth transition.
